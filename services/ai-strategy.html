THE PROBLEM

Why most AI initiatives
stall after the pilot

The pattern is always the same. A team buys licences, runs a few experiments, gets an impressive demo — and six months later nothing about how the business operates has changed. The gap isn't the technology. It's the operating model around it.

Tools get chosen before use cases. Adoption is left to enthusiasm. Nobody owns governance, so legal and compliance quietly veto everything — especially in regulated industries. And because nobody defined what success looks like, nobody can prove the investment paid off.

Strategy work only counts if it changes what your team does on a Tuesday morning. That's the standard we build to.

HOW WE THINK

The principles behind
every engagement

01

Workflows first, tools second

Buying software before mapping workflows is how tool sprawl happens. We start with how work actually flows through your business, then fit AI where it removes real friction.

02

Narrow wins beat broad ambitions

A transformation programme across ten departments will stall. One high-visibility use case, delivered end to end, builds the internal proof and appetite that makes everything after it easier.

03

Governance is an enabler

Teams without clear rules either freeze or go rogue. A pragmatic policy — what's allowed, with which data, reviewed how — is what lets a regulated business move fast with confidence.

04

AI supports decisions, people make them

We design systems where AI does the heavy lifting — drafting, analysing, monitoring — and your people keep judgment and accountability. That's what makes adoption durable and defensible.

05

Measure time-to-value, not activity

Licences issued and prompts written are vanity metrics. We define what a use case is worth before we build it — hours returned, cycle time cut, output shipped — and report against that.
