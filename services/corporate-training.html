THE PROBLEM

Why most AI training
doesn't stick

Every company has the same split: two or three power users doing remarkable things with AI, and everyone else who tried it twice, got a mediocre answer, and went back to the old way. Licences get renewed. Behaviour doesn't change.

The reason is simple: most training teaches the tool, not the job. A marketer doesn't need a lecture on how transformers work. They need to see their own campaign brief, their own reporting deck, their own inbox — done in half the time, in front of them, by them.

Adoption is the metric. Not attendance, not satisfaction scores — how many people changed how they work.

HOW WE THINK

What makes training
actually stick

01

Train the job, not the tool

Sessions are built from your team's real tasks — their briefs, their reports, their tickets. When people see their own work transformed, adoption stops being a request and becomes a pull.

02

Hands on keyboards, always

Nobody learns AI by watching someone else use it. Every workshop has participants building from the first ten minutes, leaving with working outputs — not notes about outputs.

03

Champions beat mandates

Top-down AI mandates breed quiet resistance. A respected colleague showing their workflow at a team meeting converts more people than any policy memo. We build that peer engine deliberately.

04

Reinforcement is the programme

The workshop is the start, not the deliverable. Habits form in the weeks after — which is why office hours, follow-ups, and the shared library are part of every engagement, not an upsell.

05

Judgment stays human

We teach teams to treat AI as a capable junior: fast, tireless, and always reviewed. That framing keeps quality high, keeps compliance comfortable, and keeps people invested rather than threatened.
